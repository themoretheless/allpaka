# Result: the ROWS verify mapping costs 2.7% of a round, so the q3_k/q2_k branch is not worth writing

Task #27, roadmap A9 lever "MTP on MoE: `q2_k`/`q3_k` have no ROWS mapping",
DoD step (a). Series `mtp-rows-series1`, run 2026-09-25T01:32Z-01:45Z (05:32-05:45
local). Design and criteria: `preregistration.txt`, including the 01:41Z
amendment that moved the primary statistic to the verify stage; the amendment
was written with four pairs already collected, and it cuts against the
hypothesis rather than for it.

Artifacts: `provenance-series1.txt` (pinned binary `f7549dd8818472d7…`, git head
`2347752`, dirty tree by design - the diff is only the ablation switch, the
dispatch-census fix and the report wiring), `driver-series1.txt` (live
per-pair lines), `pairs-series1.txt` (one JSON per pair with every field the
statistics use, rates at full float from the report JSON), `analyze-series1.txt`
(the whole analysis, from `analyze.py`).

## What was held equal

All 28 processes: one fingerprint (`659ac1d5f208545a`), one model
(`Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-MTP-UD-Q4_K_M.gguf`, 40 MoE layers, 21.1 GiB),
PP=480/TG=32/`ALLPAKA_DRAFT_K=4` so `m = 5` rows per round, 12 rounds,
acceptance 21/48, and `mtp stream matches plain greedy: PASS` in every log of
both arms. Load averaged 4.9-10.6 over the series; no absolute rate is claimed
here (criterion 7).

Criteria 2 and 3 admitted 14 of 14 pairs: the emitted stream and the acceptance
never moved, and the per-round verify census was 813 dispatches under `rows`
against 1293 under `norows` in every pair - a gap of 480 = `3 (m-1)` per MoE
layer x 40 layers, exactly what criterion 3 requires and exactly the amount the
pre-fix tally hid.

## The measurement

The pass-level GPU counter, which does not go through the hand-maintained
tally, says the `norows` arm issued 17574 dispatches against 11814 - 5760 more,
i.e. 480 per round x 12 rounds - and spent 499.5 ms of GPU time against 511.4
ms. So merging five rows into one dispatch per expert matrix took a third of a
verify pass's dispatches away (5760 of 17574) and became 2.3% *slower*.

Per-round verify milliseconds, median of 12 rounds, `norows` over `rows`:

    primary  5' verify-stage ms, norows/rows: median 0.9732, mean -2.50% SEM 0.46 n 14, 1/14 above 1
    secondary 5 e2E speculative rate, norows/rows (all admitted): median 1.0256, mean +1.76% SEM 1.03 n 14, 11/14 above 1
    secondary 5 e2E speculative rate, norows/rows (criterion-4 clean): median 1.0234, mean +0.51% SEM 1.75 n 7, 5/7 above 1
      verify AB: n 7, median 0.9765, 1 of 7 above 1
      verify BA: n 7, median 0.9678, 0 of 7 above 1
      e2E AB: n 7, median 1.0234, 5 of 7 above 1
      e2E BA: n 7, median 1.0264, 6 of 7 above 1

    ROWS worth -2.68% by the verify stage, -2.50% by the e2E rate (positive favours ROWS)

Both orderings agree on both statistics, and the two statistics - one taken
inside the round, one end to end - agree to 0.18 points on a number neither was
tuned to. 13 of 14 pairs put the verify stage on the same side. The series
table is in `analyze-series1.txt`.

## Decision, in the tense of a result

Criterion 6 (and 6') asked whether the ROWS-per-row difference is worth more
than about 3%. It is worth 2.68% of the verify stage and 2.50% of the
speculative token rate, and it points the wrong way: the ROWS mapping is a
small consistent *loss* on the kernels that already carry it
(`matvec_q4_k_mv`, `matvec_q5_k_mv`/`q5_k`, `matvec_q6_k`,
`matvec_q5_0_mv`, `matvec_q8_0_mv`), not a gain. Therefore:

* Roadmap A9's step (b) - writing the ROWS branch for `q3_k`/`q2_k` - is
  dropped. There is nothing to transfer: the mapping it would copy does not pay
  on the kernels that have it.
* The retire branch of criterion 6 (ROWS is itself a loss above 3%, delete it
  from the kernels that carry it) missed its threshold by 0.3 points and is
  NOT opened. Retiring the mapping buys ~2.7% of a verify round inside a
  feature that is off by default and runs at 0.8x plain decode even when on
  (`../../mtp.md`), for a change across five kernels and their pipeline
  wiring. That is churn with a payoff below the cost of being wrong about it.
  Recorded here rather than registered as a task; if MTP ever becomes the
  default path, this number is the reason to reopen it.
* The lever closes as a measured negative. The 235B's MTP return condition
  stays unreached for the reason it was already reached for - that model has no
  `nextn` block - and the `q3_k`/`q2_k` ROWS gap stops being a candidate decode
  lever.

## Why merging rows bought nothing, as the numbers force it

Five rows through one expert matrix mean five reads of that matrix's weights in
the fallback and one in ROWS, so ROWS should win by whatever the weight traffic
is worth - unless the repeats are free. They are: at this expert granularity the
per-expert slices for a round's routed set are small enough that the second to
fifth row hit cache, so the DRAM traffic the mapping merges away was never
being paid. What is left is the ROWS kernel's own overhead per round (an
extra index indirection and `m x` rows read with a token stride), which the
series prices at 2.7%. Dispatch count is confirmed to be the least of it: the
fallback issued 5760 more dispatches over a pass and finished 11.9 ms earlier.

The published per-dispatch cost reconciles the two sides of that without any
new measurement: the 30B skip control puts ~0.47 us of non-kernel cost on a
dispatch (`../../decode-opts.md`, the `R = 1.02` paragraph), so the 5760
dispatches the mapping removes are worth 2.7 ms over a 12-round pass, while the
verify stage itself moved 0.86 ms per round x 12 = 10.3 ms the other way. Merging
avoids 2.7 ms of enqueue and saves traffic that was in fact being served from
cache, and pays 10.3 ms for the ROWS kernel's own index indirection. That is the whole
2.68%, and it is why the sign is stable across 13 of 14 pairs: the two arms'
byte streams are identical by construction, so only the kernel shape differs.

Two consequences beyond this lever, both about instruments rather than kernels:

* `3m` versus `3` dispatches is not a cost on this machine at these sizes, so
  dispatch-count reductions are not a shortcut to decode time here, and
  previous reasoning that treated a lower dispatch census as progress should
  keep its timing evidence separate from its census evidence.
* The e2E rate of the MTP section is a weak meter because the section is capped
  at ~69 tokens by `new_session_mtp(spec_prompt.len() + 64 + k + 1)`
  (crates/allpaka-cli/src/bench.rs:639, :682): pairs 7 and 8 swing the
  speculative rate by -10% while their verify stage stays flat to 0.7%. Any
  future MTP A/B either prices the stage it changes or raises that ceiling
  first; this one did the former.

## Where this question should have stopped earlier

`docs/mtp.md:90-92`, committed 2026-08-28 with the MTP feature (`0dfce0d`),
already said it: `ROWS-батчинг экспертов диспатчи режет, но время не двигает`.
Roadmap A9 nevertheless opened this lever as "found by reading the code, not by
measuring" and offered step (b) as new work, without reading the line the repo
had been carrying for four weeks. The grep is cheaper than the 75 minutes the
series took, and this is the second time in this goal that a lever was priced
after being written down as already priced. What the series still buys is the
difference between an unsourced remark and a result: the old line said "does not
move the time", which is neither a number nor a sign, and the measurement says
it costs 2.68% with 13 of 14 pairs on one side - a sign that could only have
come from running it, and the thing that decides whether the existing mapping
gets retired or left alone.

## Limits of transfer

This prices the mapping on q4_k/q5_k/q6_k experts. The 235B's experts are
q2_k/q3_k, whose unpack chain is 1.86-2.01x the q4_k one, so a merged row would
save relatively more unpacking there - which is the one argument for the branch
this series cannot answer. No local model can: the 235B has no `nextn` block, so
`encode_verify_tokens` never runs on it, and the only nextn model here keeps
its experts above q3_k. If the branch is ever revisited, the honest route is a
q3_k re-quantisation of this model's experts to make the case testable, not the
kernel written first. Given 2.7% against the mapping on the kernels that carry
it, that experiment is not worth its own cost now.

    cargo build --release
    python3 docs/benchmarks/2026-09-25-mtp-rows-ablation/drive.py --pairs 14
    python3 docs/benchmarks/2026-09-25-mtp-rows-ablation/analyze.py <the printed out dir>

`ALLPAKA_NO_EXPERT_ROWS=1` is an ablation switch for this comparison, not a
lever; it is recorded as such in `docs/decode-opts.md`.

## What carries forward as a check

Two things shipped with this ablation, neither of which is a kernel: the verify
stage's `dispatched` tally now follows the branch that ran (the fix is what let
criterion 3 see 813 against 1293 at all; before it both arms printed 1293), and
the bench report writes the MTP section's two rates into the report JSON as
`mtp-speculative` / `mtp-plain` with the round and GPU phases attached, so a
paired A/B reads them at full float instead of parsing stdout.

The standing regression check for the tally is criterion 3 of this analysis, not
a new test: one pair is enough. `python3 drive.py --pairs 1` and then
`python3 analyze.py <out>` must print `1 of 1 pairs admitted` - which requires
the census gap to be a positive multiple of `3 (m-1)` per MoE layer with
`norows` larger, and the two arms to emit the same stream and the same
acceptance. If the tally ever reverts to the fixed `4 + 3m`, criterion 3 fails
the pair instead of the series silently losing its only arm discriminator. Two
minutes of GPU time; run it after touching `encode_verify_tokens`.
