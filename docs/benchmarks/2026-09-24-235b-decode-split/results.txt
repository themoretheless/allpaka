# 3. What that says, in order of size

  - The 235B's decode token has TWO expert stages that behave oppositely:
    q2_k gate/up runs at 0.99 of its own indexed bench rate (nothing left to
    find there beyond the bytes themselves), while q3_k down runs at 2.09x it.
    `down` alone is 15.95-16.22 ms of a 42.4 ms token = 37-38% of the token,
    the single largest line in the model, and the goal's named leftover.
  - Non-byte stages cost 3.5-3.9 ms/token (resnorm 2.44, router 1.71, attend
    0.69, combine 0.34, swiglu 0.04) and the census prices them at ~0.2. Router
    at 8.5x its byte price is per-dispatch latency over 94 layers, not traffic.
    This is where the skip differential's unexplained 3.6 ms of interaction
    partly lives - and it is a scheduling/latency target, not a kernel one.
  - Cross-check against the removal probe: gate_up+down+swiglu+combine = 28.1
    against the differential's independently bracketed 27.3 ms for the whole
    experts group, i.e. the two instruments agree to 3% on the total. qkv+wo =
    9.6 against the removal probe's 7.16, and the differential already showed
    that a single-group removal under-counts by ~8-9% (non-additivity), so the
    direction is as expected.

# 4. The open question, and the run that decides it

  A stage boundary bills the stall that DELAYS a stage to the stage itself. If
  the barrier in front of `down` is what the counter is charging, then the 2.09x
  is an attribution artifact, not a kernel deficit, and the q3_k chain keeps its
  previous (smaller) price. Test and thresholds are Amendment-equivalent section
  "RUN 2" of preregistration.txt: split mode with ALLPAKA_SKIP=barf (ffn-chain
  barriers) and ALLPAKA_SKIP=bar (every barrier), each pair bracketed by its own
  split-mode baseline. down <= 10 ms with gate_up+down preserved => attribution;
  down >= 14 ms with the ffn barriers gone => real kernel time; between is
  reported unresolved. Corrupted-value bias is stated in the pre-registration
  because dropping barriers garbage the activations, and garbage routing can move
  the token cost by itself (differential section 5). Resolved in section 5.

## 5.3 The control that makes the null mean something

  A null from a probe is only a null if the probe can bite. c1-head runs split
  mode with ALLPAKA_SKIP=head: its `head` label reads 0.000 while every other
  label sits at the clean value (resnorm 2.450, qkv 5.206, gate_up 11.843,
  down 16.037). So probe_skip does fire in split mode and the label table does
  respond to a skip. `bar` therefore really was applied and really cost 0.0 ms.

## 5.4 Verdict, against the pre-registered thresholds

  down is 16.04-16.06 ms/token with the ffn-chain barriers gone and 16.00-16.04
  with every barrier gone. Both are far above the 14 ms threshold, and
  gate_up+down stays at 27.9 ± 0.05 ms in all four arms, so nothing was moved
  between labels instead of removed. The barrier-attribution hypothesis is
  dead: the q3_k `down` stage costs 2.09x its own indexed, per_wait=1,
  same-shape micro-bench rate as KERNEL time, at 37% of the token. The ledger's
  q3_k row carries that blame.

  Corollary, worth having on its own: the intra-token memory_barrier_with_
  resources fences are free on this path - 1318 dispatches/token, every one of
  them still ordered, at Δ0.0 ms. The Mac-safety argument against device-wide
  sync was never about these encoder-level fences.

  Instrument caveat found on the way: the last one or two rows of a label table
  (argmax always, head once) can read 0.000 without being skipped - the sample
  buffer is fixed at MAX_SPLIT_SAMPLES = 2048. A 0.000 on a label you did not
  skip is truncation, not a measurement; the sum/exec residual (1.7-1.8 ms/token
  in clean split runs) is the place to look for unlabelled time.

    k2   68.75 / 61.72      7.03    45.00              0.000      0.000

  CONTROL PASSES: the skipped stages read exactly 0.000 in both reps and the
  expert stages do not absorb their time - k2, whose three runs are all inside
  one mildly degraded window, moves gate_up by +0.197 ms and down by +0.289 ms
  while 10.58 ms/token of q2_k attention traffic disappears. k1 is the same
  story against a wild bracket (its base2 hit 106.56 ms/token, the worst single
  run seen on this machine): the arm's down is 16.083 against a baseline mean of
  25.709, i.e. the arm is clean and the baseline is not.

  Two things follow.

  1. `bar` and `barf` really were applied. A skip in this function moves its own
     label to zero and nothing else, so the barrier null of 5.1 is a measurement
     and not instrument silence - which matters because the same instrument did
     go silent once already (the dispatch-counter coverage gap).
  2. The `down` deficit is not interference from neighbouring stages. Removing
     a quarter of the token's q2_k traffic leaves it at 16.0 ms. Whatever costs
     2.09x is inside the down dispatch itself or in how the harness measures it,
     which is the question 2026-09-24-indexed-feed-scatter now answers with the
     feed, slot-layout and activation-layout axes.

# 6. Annotation added 2026-09-25 - the 2.09x is now named, and it was not a kernel

  Every `down` price in sections 2-5 of this file was measured under the default
  that was live then: `ALLPAKA_SWFUSE` folded `silu(gate)*up` into the down
  activation load. That fold re-applies silu once per output ROW, so the stage
  this file priced at 15.95-16.22 ms/token was recomputing the same 12 288
  activations 4096 times per expert.

  The follow-up measurement is
  [`../2026-09-24-235b-swfuse/results.txt`](../2026-09-24-235b-swfuse/results.txt)
  sections 3 and 12. Its numbers close this file's open question:

  - the fused q3_k indexed [4096,1536] x8 arm benches at 108.9 GB/s against its
    plain arm's 222.0, i.e. 0.491x;
  - the census's plain-rate prediction of 7.69 ms divided by that 0.491 is
    15.66 ms, against 15.95-16.22 measured here (16.01 in section 3 of the
    artifact above, the same stage) - 0.3-0.6 ms, two to four clock quanta of
    0.156 ms on a 16 ms stage. Stated as rates the same closure reads in-situ
    106.8 GB/s against the bench fused arm's 108.9, i.e. 0.98x;
  - so section 5.6's point 2 ("whatever costs 2.09x is inside the down dispatch
    itself or in how the harness measures it") resolves to: neither. The 2.09x
    was the scheduling policy, and this file's instrument was right.

  What survives of the q3_k price is format, not policy: plain-vs-plain the
  kernel is 222.0 against q4_k's 468.8 GB/s = 0.474x, i.e. the unpack chain
  still costs 2.11x per byte, and at the 1.71 GB the model reads that is ~4.5 ms
  of the current 35.0 ms token rather than 8.3.

  Numbers in this file that are stale by that policy change and must not be
  quoted as the model's price any more: the 42.3-42.4 ms/token baselines (the
  shipped binary's is 35.0, see section 12 of the artifact above), the `down`
  row of every per-stage table, and therefore any share computed off them. The
  barrier nulls (5.1), the zero-label skip controls (5.6), the gate_up/qkv/wo/
  head ratios and the dispatch-count census are unaffected - none of them reads
  the folded stage as its subject.
