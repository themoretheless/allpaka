# 2x2 fold/layout harness - two attempts, both rejected by their own gate

Task #25's pre-flight measurement, run 2026-09-25 02:25 local (22:25 UTC), twice,
90 seconds apart. It answered nothing. It is filed because the first attempt came
within one decimal place of supporting a conclusion, and only the second run showed
that the quantity the pre-registration cared about - the fold/plain RATIO - carries
the window's state just as the absolute rate does.

Harness, verbatim, ~13-15 s per attempt:

    ALLPAKA_BENCH_FEEDS=1 cargo test --release -p allpaka-backend --test gpu_ffnbench \
      -- q35_down_shape --ignored --nocapture

`gpu_ffnbench.rs::q35_down_shape_priced_fused_and_plain_across_x_layouts`: four shapes
x four arms (activation x shared / per-slot  x  fold off / on), x8 expert dispatch,
384 MB streamed per shape, best of 4 interleaved feeds, and two of the four shapes are
published meters whose absolute rate is the admission gate (q4_k [4096,1536] 468.8,
q5_k [4096,1536] 344.9, gate = within 3%).

## Verdict

REJECTED, both attempts, no conclusion of any kind. The 35B's fold cost stays at
0.23-0.64 ms/token, the band the pre-registration carries, and the shape question
stays open.

## The instrument law this buys

**A within-run ratio is not automatically regime-robust.** The repo already holds that
an absolute GB/s read under contention is worthless, and that in-process rotated ratio
harnesses survive a busy CPU. This is the counterexample on the ratio side: a fold/plain
ratio measured between two arms of one process, interleaved, at 1.003x repeatability,
moved 34% between two such processes 90 s apart. Contention is therefore not only
offsetting the level here; it is changing the relative cost of the two arms, which is
what a ratio is supposed to be immune to. No mechanism is offered for why, and the
obvious one is contradicted by these same two runs: "a slower window is DRAM-saturated,
so the fold's extra ALU becomes free and the ratio approaches 1" predicts attempt 2 to
read CLOSER to 1 than attempt 1, and it read farther (0.556 vs 0.845 at 0.51x vs 0.86x
of meter). Whatever the window does to the fold, it is not monotone in window speed, and
settling that is not this artifact's job. The consequence is procedural: a ratio arm
needs the absolute meter gate exactly as a rate arm does, plus at least two independent
processes of it, because within-process repeatability - 1.003x here, perfect - is the
last thing contention corrupts. That also retroactively explains why the first narrow-shape
attempt at 0.40x of meter printed a non-monotonic rows axis
([`../2026-09-25-narrow-shape-axes/results.txt`](../2026-09-25-narrow-shape-axes/results.txt))
- the two artifacts are the same failure, one seen in a rate axis and one in a ratio.

## What a future attempt must do differently

  - Gate on the meter AND require two processes of the harness whose fold ratios agree
    within 5%, and report the ratio of the *control* shape as the window's own
    temperature. Only one meter gate is not enough for a ratio claim.
  - Capture the full stdout to a file, not through a grep, so a rejected attempt stays
    recomputable. Attempt 2 survives only as filtered lines - its per-arm best-of-4
    values are gone, which is why the ratios above are quoted to three decimals from
    the printed GB/s and nothing more can be extracted from them.
