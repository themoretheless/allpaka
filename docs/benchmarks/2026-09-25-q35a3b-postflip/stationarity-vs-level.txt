# A ratio reproduces when the window's LEVEL is stationary, not when it is quiet

Task #25's pre-flight, run 2026-09-25 04:51-04:58 local (00:51-00:58 UTC) by
[`preflight.py`](preflight.py). Three attempts, all three rejected by their own
gates - and the rejection is the interesting part, because
[`harness-rejected.txt`](harness-rejected.txt) had just concluded that a fold/plain
ratio is not regime-robust, and these three processes show it reproducing to under
two percent at a level 25% below the published meter.

Machine state while it ran: 1-minute load 5.8-7.0 with no single hog (`remoting_me2me_host`
51%, `WindowServer` 31%, `opener-gui` 31%, three Qoder helpers, Kimi, rider, Cursor,
a VM), `vm.swapusage` 16 965 of 18 432 MiB used with 1 467 free. That is a desktop in
use, not an idle machine, and the byte rate is the thing it moves.

The load average is not what separates the two states either: attempts 4-9 ran at load
4.27-5.19, inside the ceiling the wall runs use, and read the same 0.75x as attempt 1 at
6.99. Whatever holds this machine 25% down on the byte pipe, `vm.loadavg` does not see it.

So the law that `harness-rejected.txt` recorded is incomplete, and this is the
correction: **what a ratio needs is a stationary level, not a quiet one.** The
repeater test in `run-pairs.sh` (the `plain2` arm) was already built on that
principle for wall rates; gate A of the pre-flight (meters within 3% of published)
tests a different question - whether the machine is in the state the published
constants describe - and this window says that question is not the same as
"will these two processes agree".

Gate B is not redundant with C either, and these rows show why: 8 of the 10 processes
had at least one arm whose own four-pass median spread exceeded 1.03x, peaking at
5.591x, while the ratios taken from those same arms' best-of-4 stayed inside a 5%
band. That is the caution this adds to its own finding: **best-of-4 is a maximum, and
a maximum is the statistic interference moves least.** A window can therefore look
ratio-reproducible on the surviving statistic while its median pass is badly
disturbed, which is exactly what a 5.591x internal spread sitting next to an
0.847 down-ratio means. The repeatability claimed above is a statement about maxima
of four, not about the passes in between.



## What the level does to a ratio is not monotone, so it cannot be corrected

`q4_k`'s x-shared ratio is 0.512 at 1.00x, 0.545-0.567 at 0.75-0.83x, and 0.445 at
0.51x - it rises as the machine slows and then falls off. `q5_k`'s is 0.733 at 1.00x
against 0.770-0.773 at 0.80x (a +5.5% bias on the very value the 35B's band was built
from) but 0.719 in the one process that read it at 0.83x - so even two processes at
nearly the same level disagree by 6.7% on that row, which is why the claim above is
that stationarity BOUNDS a ratio's repeatability rather than guaranteeing it. There
is no curve to interpolate and no correction to apply; a depressed window gives a
different number, and only the absolute meter gate says whether it is the number
being published about. That is why gate A stays.

The mechanism is visible in which arm moves: at 0.75x the `q5_k` plain arm reads
276.0 against its published 344.9 (-20.0%) while the fused arm reads 213.0 against
252.8 (-15.7%). The fold's extra work is partly free when the machine is the bottleneck.

## What this window would have said, had it been admissible, and why it still matters

The 35B's own down geometry [2048,512] reads a per-slot fold/plain ratio averaging
0.845 across ten processes (0.813-0.856), against its format's meter shape at
0.773 (0.763-0.790) - i.e. the fold is ~9% *cheaper* at the narrow shape than at the
shape the pre-registration borrowed (0.733). Put through the pre-registered formula
`saving = t_plain x (1/r - 1)` with `t_plain = 203.5 MiB / 345 GB/s = 0.62 ms`:

    at r = 0.845 (this window, applied to a settled-window t_plain)  0.113 ms/token
    at r = 0.845, t_plain recomputed at this window's own 276 GB/s   0.142 ms/token
    at r = 0.733 (the borrowed settled ratio)                        0.23 ms/token  = the band's low end

Two consequences, neither of which needs the window to be admissible:

1. The band's *low* end was the honest one and the +6.5% high end came from the
   0.491 `q3_k` ratio, which is a different format's ALU load. A shape-corrected
   prediction sits at 0.113-0.142 ms/token.
2. 0.113-0.142 ms/token is **below what the 10-pair wall harness resolves** (that
   instrument's own SEM on a settled window is 0.083-0.240 ms/token). This is the
   number the four admitted pairs of run1 actually printed: mean 0.1195, SEM 0.1566.
   The pilot's 0.548 from the same binary in a worse window is the outlier.

So the pre-flight is no longer telling the wall run what to expect in a way that
changes its DoD - ten admitted pairs are still what decides whether this model's
published decode row moves - but it does say what "below the instrument" will mean
if that is the verdict: a ~1% fold benefit on a model where the fold touches 8.1%
of the decode bytes, not the 2.3-6.5% the band advertised.

## DoD for this finding

Admissible only as an instrument statement about ratio repeatability across
processes at a fixed level, from the six rows above; the fold cost of the 35B is
NOT a result here and stays 0.23-0.64 ms/token in the pre-registration until either
a gate-A-passing pre-flight or the ten wall pairs say otherwise. To promote it,
one settled window (`q4_k` plain within 3% of 468.8) of the same 15 s harness,
two processes, which would price the shape correction with no bias term at all.
