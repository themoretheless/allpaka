B4 RESULT - the in-tree `packed_char4` qs loads: null at |<=1.7 %|, shipped form kept
=====================================================================================

Artifact for [`preregistration.txt`](preregistration.txt). It closes row B4 of the
2026-09-26 era audit of `docs/decode-opts.md`'s 2026-09-12 dead-end block - the last
class-B item whose evidence was a live default-build kernel path priced on a
last-level-cache-resident meter. Campaign ran 2026-09-26 03:19-03:31 UTC; 12 processes,
10 admitted, 4 shared round-pairs, 9.1 min of benchmark wall time.

VERDICT
-------

**Null. The vectorized (`packed_char4`) loads stay, and the row's wording changes from
"micro noise, kept, no regress expected" to "measured on the streamed instrument: no
effect above +-1.7 %".**

The decision rule needed `scalar` to beat `packed` by >3 % on the median meter-ratio of
*both* q8_0 rows, with a consistent sign in >=4 shared rounds. It did not: the
pre-registered estimator reads -1.03 % and -0.81 % (i.e. `packed` ahead, under the bar),
and the sign is positive in 1 of 4 and 2 of 4 rounds. The paired absolute estimator -
the right one here, for the reason given in the last section - reads **-0.63 %** on the
wide row and **+0.37 %** on the GLM row, so the two rows disagree in sign with every
within-round |delta| at or under 1.68 %. The pre-registration's tiebreak applies: where
the rows disagree, the verdict follows the wide row (GLM's shared down and the dense
models' rows dispatch at that shape), which reads +0.37 %, three of four rounds
positive, still 8x under the bar.

1. What the question actually was
---------------------------------

The acceptance note in the tree read "micro noise vs scalar (~509 vs ~518 GB/s); kept
(matches `matvec_q8_0` style, no regress expected)". Its real open question was not the
size of the old number but its *kind*: at 34 B per Q8_0 block, packing 32 quants into 8
alignment-1 `packed_char4` loads cuts memory *ops* 4x while moving identical bytes.
Whether that can matter at all depends on whether the stage is latency-bound or
bandwidth-bound - and the LL-small working sets that priced it in 2026-09-12 could not
distinguish those, because a cache-resident stage is latency-bound by construction.

Answer, measured: **bandwidth-bound, at the wall.** Both arms and both q8_0 rows in the
shipped cell (feed 8, plain) - 20 readings over the 10 admitted processes on a ~384 MiB
streamed working set - lie between 498.8 and 509.2 GB/s, a 2.1 % envelope containing
both arms; widened to all four cells it is 488.3-509.2 GB/s, 4.2 %, and the whole of the
extra is the fused cells' known swiglu cost, which both arms pay identically. A stage
pinned at the byte ceiling has no op-issue slack for a load-width change to convert into,
which is why the effect is where the byte-pipe argument predicts it should be, at zero.
The
old "~509 vs ~518" gap is inside that envelope: it was never a 1.8 % delta, it was two
readings of the same wall.

3. The correctness gate caught a wrong arm
------------------------------------------

Before any timing round: `cargo test -p allpaka-backend q8_0` in the copy. The first
revert read `device const uchar* qb = blk + 2;` and `q8_0_matches_cpu_reference` went
red on element zero - gpu 7.993707 vs cpu -3.144671 - because `packed_char4`'s
components are *signed* int8, so byte-addressing the same memory as uchar shifts every
negative quant by +256. Fixed to `(device const char*)`; after that the copy's whole
10-test `gpu_parity` suite passes, including `q8_0_matches_cpu_reference` (the tile
site) and `indexed_matvec_matches_cpu_reference` (the `_mv` site). No timing round ran
against the numerically wrong build. Had this lever been priced without the gate, the
"scalar is 4x wrong" arm would have been measured as a giant win for `packed`.

5. Method finding: a meter is only a normalizer if it is the *quieter* channel
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The pre-registered estimator divides each q8_0 row by the same process's `q6_k down`
meter, on the standing rule that two processes minutes apart cannot be compared on
absolute rates. Here that rule costs more than it buys. Across the admitted processes
the shipped-cell row varies by sd 1.5-1.8 GB/s (spread 0.7-1.7 %), while the *meter*
varies by sd 5.9-20.6 GB/s (spread 3.3-8.4 %) - r3's scalar process meter fell to 458.1
and r4's to 429.6 while that process's q8_0 row stayed at 506.2 and 505.0, i.e. dead on
the wall. Dividing a pinned quantity by a wandering one manufactures the very variance
it was meant to cancel: the ratio estimator's headline for `scalar` (-1.03 %, 1/4
rounds positive) is mostly r3's and r1's meter, not the kernel's.

That is not an argument to drop the meter - it is the thing that catches a
whole-machine slowdown, and gates 2-4 need it. It is an argument to check *which* of the
two channels is tighter before normalizing, and to publish the paired absolute deltas
next to the ratio verdict whenever the measured row is at a byte-pipe wall. Had this
campaign run on ratios alone, the sign of a 1 % effect on a null lever would have been
reported as a fact about `packed_char4`.

6. What is not claimed
----------------------

- Not an e2E claim. A +-1 % micro effect on Q8_0 converts to tokens through Q8_0's share
  of a model's per-token bytes, and nothing here is above the instrument's own 1.7 %
  paired envelope, so there is nothing to carry.
- Not a claim about the *tile* path's real workload: `matvec_q8_0` runs at prefill, and
  the pre-registered cells are the indexed decode shapes. The tile site was reverted and
  correctness-gated with the `_mv` site, and its row is the `[4096,1408]`-class one, but a
  prefill-side conclusion is out of scope.
- Not a claim about other formats' load width: `q2_k`'s word loads and `q5_0`'s ushort
  pairs are separate code with separate evidence.
- Not a general "load count never matters" result - the same repo already has the
  counter-case. In `q3_k`, changing the block-row width by padding to 112 measured
  **-4.6 %** (docs/decode-opts.md's per-block budget line), and that stage is not at the
  byte wall. The claim here is narrower and depends on this measurement: *at the wall*,
  as both q8_0 arms demonstrably were in every cell, op count has no slack to convert.
- n = 4 shared round-pairs, the pre-registration's minimum, reached only because both
  vetoed processes were `scalar` arms in r4/r5. A null with four pairs and a 1.7 %
  envelope bounds the effect at roughly 2x the largest observed swing; it does not
  resolve a 1 % difference in either direction, and no future decision in this repo should
  cite this artifact for one.
