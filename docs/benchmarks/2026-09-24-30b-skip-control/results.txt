qwen3-30b-a3b Q4_K_M: bracketed ALLPAKA_SKIP control for the 235B skip differential
====================================================================================

Run 2026-09-24 19:17-19:26 UTC, three reps of every arm, each arm immediately
preceded by its own baseline (18 bracketed baselines in total). Purpose: test
the pre-registered fork from preregistration.txt - is the gap between the streamed
byte model and measured decode cost a UNIFORM in-situ inflation (all groups
share one R), or FORMAT-SPECIFIC (only the K-quant experts inflate)? The 30B is
the control because its experts are q4_k and its baselines bracket to ~1%
whereas the 235B window was +/-38%.

# 4. GPU-executing vs wall: the 30B is 95% closed, and dispatch overhead is tiny

 baseline: 403-406 ms "gpu clock during decode: executing" over 64 tokens
   = 6.30-6.34 ms/token inside kernels of a 6.68 ms/token wall -> closure 0.946.
 all arm : 36 ms / 64 = 0.56 ms inside kernels of 0.74 ms wall, at 386
   dispatches/token (24704/64) vs the baseline's 674 (43136/64).
 => the non-kernel residue of a decode token on this model is ~0.18 ms with 386
    dispatches still firing, i.e. ~0.47 us per dispatch, NOT a per-dispatch cost
    anywhere near microseconds. Projected onto the 235B's 1318 dispatches/token
    that is 0.62 ms, so multi-encoder/dispatch overhead cannot account for the
    235B's 12.6 ms of rate-sum shortfall. That hypothesis is dead on arithmetic
    from this control.

# 5. Instrument finding (independent of the fork)

 The "gpu during decode: N dispatches" counter does NOT see the qkv, attend or
 head dispatches: skipping those three groups leaves the total pinned at exactly
 43136 for all six runs, while the wall time they save is reproducible to
 0.01-0.17 ms across three reps. It does see experts (43136 -> 30848), router
 (-> 36992) and all (-> 24704). So a Delta-0 dispatch reading on those three
 groups is a counter-coverage artifact, not a dead probe - this is the same
 conclusion the 235B run reached by a different route (2026-09-24-235b-skip-differential/
 results.txt sections 1 and 6), now confirmed with 3/3 versus 0/3
 instead of one rep.

# 6. Reading: the fork goes to FORMAT-SPECIFIC, with one caveat

 - experts (q4_k here): R = 1.02. On a clean bracketed window the streamed byte
   model predicts the expert group's real cost to within 2%. There is no global
   utilisation factor on this model.
 - qkv: R = 1.18, head: R = 0.72, attend: no P (KV reads, not GGUF roles). These
   are the small/latency-bound shapes, and they are where the 30B's own inflation
   sits (sum of parts 5.38 vs `all` 5.95 - the extra 0.57 ms is the inter-kernel
   gap that `all` also removes).
 - The 235B's R(experts) = 1.32 was measured on q2_k/q3_k experts, i.e. the
   formats the census rates came from, and it is the only group there that large.
   The 30B control says a q4_k expert group does NOT inflate, which is what the
   "K-quant-specific inflation" branch of the fork predicts and what a uniform
   utilisation factor would not.
 - Caveat, and it is load-bearing: the 235B's 1.32 came from an UNBRACKETED
   single rep inside a +/-38% window, so the comparison above is a clean control
   against a dirty measurement. The fork is not resolved until the 235B gets the
   same bracketed mini-run. See roadmap A9 task and 235B results.txt section 4.

# 7. Negative results recorded here

 - Skipping the router does not remove ~0.10 ms, it ADDS ~6.95 ms: 13.55-13.66
   ms/token against a 6.66-6.68 bracket, 3/3 reproducible, GPU-executing also
   doubles (850 ms vs ~404 ms over the run) while 6144 dispatches over the run (96/token)
   disappear. Removing the router corrupts the top-k slots, so the indexed expert
   gather then reads the wrong (larger or uncached) expert blocks. Any removal
   probe that touches anything feeding the indexed gather measures a corrupted
   token, not a cheaper one - arm isolation, not speed.
 - The literal ALLPAKA_SKIP value "all" matches no group and silently runs the
   full model; the 235B run's "all" arm was therefore a duplicate baseline (and
   is what revealed the +/-38% window). This control uses the comma list.
