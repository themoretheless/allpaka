## Verdict

`ALLPAKA_Q5_0_NR0` **stays at 2**. Neither challenger reaches the pre-registered +3 %
on the shipped cell, and the one that matters for GLM is a regression:

  row                       NR0=1 vs 2      NR0=4 vs 2     (plain, feed=8, shipped cell)
  q5_0 down   [4096,1536]       -4.45 %          -1.03 %
  q5_0 glm down [4096,1408]     -7.86 %          +0.46 %

Those are within-round paired medians (arm / default inside the same round, median over
the rounds where both ran): NR0=1 n=5 rounds, every pair negative on both rows; NR0=4 n=4
rounds, three of four negative on both rows. Under the driver's own rule (median over
pooled admitted rounds) the same data reads -4.76 %/-6.63 % and -1.22 %/-2.30 % with
0/3 and 1/3 rounds positive. Both estimators say `hold`; the rule's flip condition
(>3 % on both rows, sign positive in >=4 shared rounds) is not met by either arm under
either reading, so no parity arm and no e2E A/B is owed.

This was the family's last unswept geometry and it was not sweepable until this series:
`lanes_per_row` returned a fixed 16 lanes for `Q5_0` with the `_mv` kernel on, while the
kernel itself derives `NR0 = 32 / LPR` from the function constant, exactly like the
already-swept `q8_0_mv`. The knob is now `ALLPAKA_Q5_0_NR0` in {1,2,4}, default 2, and the
routing unit test `indexed_mv_routing_respects_rows_per_simd_group` carries a `q5_0` entry
at each arm; `matvec_q5_0_mv` takes part in
`gpu_parity::indexed_matvec_matches_cpu_reference`, which is mutation-verified against the
`n_out % NR0` guard, so the sweep cannot silently out-run the indexed geometry contract.

## Every cell, all four

Median meter-ratio of arm over default within the same round, feed=8:

  row                   cell     NR0=1        NR0=4
  q5_0 down             plain    -4.45 %      -1.03 %
  q5_0 down             fused   -18.00 %      +4.87 %
  q5_0 glm down         plain    -7.86 %      +0.46 %
  q5_0 glm down         fused   -20.15 %      +6.55 %

The fusion reversal found for `q4_k` and `q8_0` in
[the 09-25 series](../2026-09-25-nr0-indexed-remeasure/results.txt) repeats here, and
sharper: a fused down recomputes `silu(gate) * up` for every row that loads the
activation, and NR0 is how many rows share one load, so the fused penalty runs ~1/NR0 -
at NR0=1 it costs q5_0 18-20 %, at NR0=4 it is paid back and overpaid by +5-6.6 %. Three
formats now agree on the sign and the size of the term, which is the strongest
mechanistic result this instrument has produced. It does not change what ships:
`ALLPAKA_SWFUSE` is opt-in and off, so the shipped cell is the plain one, and there NR0=2
wins or ties. If SWFUSE ever flips, NR0=4 becomes `q5_0`'s paired choice and should be
re-measured with that conjunction rather than inherited.

## What the four gates did not catch, and what caught it

39 processes, 22 admitted: 10 spread vetoes, 5 meter vetoes, 2 load vetoes, max loadavg
10.7. Every admitted process in rounds 1-3, 5-7 and 10 reads 370-398 GB/s on `q5_0 down`;
from round 8 onward six admitted processes read 283-331 (r8/2, r9/1, r9/2, r12/4 and all
three of r13), and the round-4, round-11 and other round-8/12 processes - all of which the
gates removed - read 225-317. In the slow processes the machine was not busy by the
instrument's own measures: load 1.6-3.1, best/med under 1.05, and the `q6_k` meter slid
only to 432-463, still inside gate 2's 424.4 band - while the q5_0 rows fell 25-27 %. So
the two rows degraded unequally by roughly 2x, which is what breaks a ratio-to-meter
metric: gate 3 cannot see a *uniform* slowdown of one row (its spread stays clean), and
gate 2 watches only the meter, which is read earlier in the same process than the q5_0
rows are. With the working set partly paging, position in the harness's format order
decides who pays - the run reads its formats one after another, 384-512 MB each, and
swap was 9.3 of 10 GB used with 295k pageouts while `vm_stat` still reported 80 % of RAM
free.

Two defences, both applied to every record and reported:

1. Row-absolute floor. `analyze-nr0.py --floor 0.85 --clean r1,r2,r3,r5,r6,r7,r10` reads
   the reference off the measured rows themselves (381.4 and 363.5 GB/s, the admitted
   median in the pre-shift window) and drops any process whose shipped-cell best falls
   under 85 % of it. It removes 6 of the 22 admitted records - 2/r8, 1/r9, 4/r12 and all
   three of r13 - symmetrically across arms, including records that favour the default
   (r8/2, r9/1) and records that favour a challenger (r12/4, r13/4). This gate was
   declared after the rounds it filters; the unfiltered verdict is printed above as well,
   and it points the same way.
2. Within-round pairing, which is what the numbers in the Verdict section use. The pooled
   median-of-ratios is what let the shift read as a flip in the first place: pooled over
   all 22 admitted records the default's median drops below NR0=4's, and the driver's own
   line came out `NR0=4 +5.50 %/+5.04 %`. Pairing inside the round cancels the machine
   state; the floor then removes pairs whose partner is gone.

The single record that would otherwise have been read as a win is round 9's NR0=4 process:
+13.9 % and +15.0 % against the default in that round, the only positive pair NR0=4 has on
`q5_0 glm down` above +2 %. Its order in the round was 4, then 1, then 2, and the last two
sat at 0.668 and 0.688 while the first sat at 0.784 - i.e. the round degraded after the
first process, and the "win" is position in the round, not rows per SIMD group. Rotation
across rounds is what the design uses to average such drift, and with three processes per
round a step is not averaged: it is transferred to whichever arm happens to run first. A
series this long should rotate *within* the round as well, or gate on the row absolutes.

## What is not claimed

- No e2E number. q5_0 is GLM-4.5-Air's expert-down format and ~12 % of the bytes a token
  reads there, so the sweep's spread on the row GLM dispatches (<=1.5 %) converts to
  <=0.2 % of decode - far below the +-2-4 % per-pair noise that made the 09-24 e2E runs
  need 32 pairs. Spending that campaign on a 1.5 % micro difference is not worth it, and
  this file does not pretend otherwise.
- Not a llama.cpp parity claim; no llama arm ran in this sequence.
- Not a claim that 2 is optimal at *every* geometry: only the two q5_0 shapes the harness
  carries were measured, both indexed x8, which is what a MoE decode issues.
- The NR0=4 fused-cell win is an ordering within a configuration that does not ship.
